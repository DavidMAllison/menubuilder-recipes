# Rafute

## Ingredients

- 2 lb pork belly block ((boneless, skin on))
- 4 cups water
- 2 cups katsuobushi (dried bonito flakes) ((packed))
- 1 cup awamori (Okinawan distilled liquor) ((you can substitute it with sake or vodka))
- ½ cup black sugar
- ⅓ cup soy sauce ((5 Tbsp, divided))
- 1 knob ginger ((1 inch, 2.5 cm))

## Instructions

1. Assemble all necessary ingredients.
2. Position a 2 lb pork belly block skin-side up in a large pot, then add water until the pork is completely submerged and heat until boiling.
3. Maintain a boil for 1 minute, then drain the water completely.
4. Wash away all visible scum and foam from the pork belly using cold running water. Clean out the pot thoroughly, return the pork belly, and cover it with fresh cold water.
5. Return the pot to a boil and remove any rising scum and foam from the surface. Lower the heat to a gentle simmer and leave uncovered for 1–1½ hours minimum, allowing odors to escape. Should the water level drop below the pork, top up with boiling water as necessary. Continue skimming whenever scum appears.
6. In a separate small pot, heat 4 cups water until it reaches a boil. When boiling, stir in 2 cups katsuobushi.
7. Allow to simmer for just 15 seconds before removing from heat. Leave the katsuobushi to steep in the hot liquid undisturbed. Reserve this katsuo dashi for later use.
8. Slice 1 knob ginger into thin julienne pieces. Submerge them in water for 10–15 minutes to reduce their pungency, then drain thoroughly and keep ready as a garnish.
9. Once the pork belly has cooked for 1–1½ hours, remove it from the pot onto a plate and cover tightly with aluminum foil to prevent drying. Allow to cool until comfortable to handle. Note: The cooking liquid can be saved, strained, and frozen for use in Okinawa Soba, Champon, or other soups.
10. When the meat has cooled sufficiently, cut it into slices measuring 2–3 cm (¾–1⅕ inches) across. Transfer these slices to a heavy-bottomed pot.
11. Pour the strained katsuo dashi into the pot with the pork.
12. Add 1 cup awamori, ½ cup black sugar, and 2 Tbsp soy sauce to the pot. Consult the blog post regarding the rationale for adding soy sauce in stages rather than all at once.
13. Stir the mixture until combined and ensure the pork slices are submerged in sauce. Bring to a gentle simmer, skimming away any impurities that rise to the surface. Cover with an otoshibuta and maintain medium-low heat for 1 hour. See the post about otoshibuta for instructions on creating your own drop lid.
14. After 1 hour has passed, incorporate an additional 2 Tbsp soy sauce and continue cooking for 30 minutes.
15. After 30 more minutes, add the final 1 Tbsp soy sauce and cook for approximately 15 minutes longer until the pork reaches optimal tenderness and the skin and fat become glossy and gelatinous. Plate the pork belly and spoon some braising liquid over it. Top with the prepared ginger. Serve alongside steamed rice and vegetables, or alternatively atop Okinawa Soba.
