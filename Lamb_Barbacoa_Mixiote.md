# Lamb Barbacoa Mixiote

## Notes

A shortcut to getting the iconic Mexican roadside stand barbacoa experience at home.

## Ingredients

- 4 pounds lamb stew meat (bone in, cut into about 2-inch pieces)
- 5 teaspoons kosher or sea salt
- 8 guajillo chiles (stemmed and seeded)
- 3 pasilla chiles (stemmed and seeded)
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- ½ teaspoon dried marjoram
- ½ teaspoon dried thyme
- 4 whole cloves (tops only)
- 4 garlic cloves (peeled)
- 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
- 12 bay leaves (lightly toasted)
- 12 avocado leaves (lightly toasted, optional)
- 3 tablespoons natural rice vinegar (or white distilled vinegar)
- 12 sheets of parchment paper (approx. 12x16 inches)
- 1 cup light beer
- Nopalitos salad
- Cooked black beans
- Avocado slices
- Warm corn tortillas
- Lime wedges (for squeezing juice)
- Chopped white onion
- Chopped jalapeño
- Chopped cilantro

## Instructions

1. After rinsing the meat, dry it thoroughly by patting. Place it in a large bowl and sprinkle 4 teaspoons of salt across all the pieces, rubbing it in with your hands until everything is well coated. Set it aside while you prepare the adobo marinade.

2. Heat a comal or skillet to medium or medium-low heat. Toast the chiles by turning them often as they begin to darken and emit their pungent fragrance for 1 to 2 minutes (you may want to open a window or turn on a fan). Move them to a medium saucepan and add enough water to cover them generously. Heat to a simmer over medium heat and let them simmer gently for 12 to 15 minutes until they soften, puff up, and are completely rehydrated.

3. Transfer the rehydrated chiles along with 1 cup of their cooking water into a blender. Add the cumin, oregano, marjoram, thyme, cloves without stems, garlic, black pepper, and the remaining teaspoon of salt. Blend until the mixture reaches a completely smooth consistency. Pour the resulting marinade over your salted meat. Using your hands, massage it into the meat so that all pieces are evenly and thoroughly coated. Cover the bowl and refrigerate for anywhere between 2 and 48 hours—the longer it sits, the more pronounced the flavors become.

4. Remove the meat from its marinade when you're prepared to begin cooking. Heat a small skillet or comal to medium-low. Briefly toast the avocado leaves and bay leaves by cooking them for about 30 seconds per side. Put them to the side.

5. Divide the meat with its marinade into 5 to 6 portions of equal size. For each portion, place a sheet of parchment paper on your work surface and position the meat in the middle. Place an avocado leaf and a bay leaf on top of the meat if available. Gather all four corners of the parchment upward and bring them together to completely enclose the meat, then twist the opening closed and tie it securely with kitchen twine. Place this first packet into another parchment sheet and repeat this process with all remaining portions.

6. Fill a large pot or steamer with beer or pulque, then add water until it reaches the bottom of the steamer basket. Put a coin under the basket in the pot—when the water simmers, the coin will rattle, and when it stops making noise, you need to add more water. Position your wrapped packets in the basket with their twisted tops pointing upward. Put the lid on, turn the heat to medium, and once steam forms, cook for 2 hours. Keep checking both the sound and appearance of the liquid as it simmers.

7. Unwrap one packet to check if it's done—the meat should shred easily with a fork. Turn off the heat and let the mixiote sit undisturbed for at least 1 hour before serving. The food will remain extremely hot during this resting time.
