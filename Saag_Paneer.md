# Saag Paneer

## Notes

- Kids: pull out paneer pieces before adding the tempering — mild and fine without it.
- Mustard greens add a pleasant bitterness. Baby kale is the easiest substitute. **Measure torn leaves to exactly 4 cups** — too much makes it bitter and overpowers the dish.
- Hing (asafoetida) is optional — adds faint savory depth but the dish works without it.
- Kashmiri chili gives deep red color with mild heat — substitute regular chili flakes if unavailable.

## Ingredients

### Paneer & Greens
- 250g (9 oz) paneer, cut into cubes
- 4 cups baby spinach (palak), stems removed
- 4 cups mustard greens (or baby kale / choy sum), stems removed

### Masala Base
- 1½ tablespoons ghee (divided)
- ¾ cup onion, sliced
- 1–2 green chilies, slit (skip for kids)
- ½ inch ginger, peeled and sliced
- 3 garlic cloves, peeled
- ½ cup tomatoes, sliced
- ⅓ cup carrot (or baby turnip), sliced
- ⅛ teaspoon turmeric powder
- ¾ teaspoon coriander powder
- ½ teaspoon cumin powder
- ¼ cup yogurt
- ¼ cup water
- ½ teaspoon salt
- ¾ teaspoon garam masala
- ¼ cup heavy cream (or cashew cream from 12 cashews)
- 1 lemon, for serving

### Tempering
- 1 tablespoon unsalted butter (or ghee)
- ⅛ teaspoon hing (asafoetida)
- 1–2 garlic cloves, sliced
- 1–2 dried red chilies
- ½ teaspoon Kashmiri red chili powder (or regular chili flakes)

## Instructions

1. Rinse spinach and mustard greens at least three times. Drain in a colander. If using kale, blanch briefly in boiling water to soften, then drain.
2. Heat ½ tablespoon ghee in a non-stick pan over medium-high. Add paneer cubes and fry until golden on both sides. Set aside.
3. Heat remaining ghee in the same pan. Add onions, green chilies, ginger, and garlic. Sauté until the raw smell from the onions disappears, about 7–8 minutes.
4. Stir in coriander, turmeric, and cumin. Add tomatoes and yogurt. Mix well. Cover and cook until tomatoes break down completely and onions are soft.
5. Add carrot and sauté 2 minutes to bring out sweetness.
6. Add spinach and mustard greens. Let steam and cook until wilted, about 5–6 minutes.
7. Add water. Using an immersion blender (or regular blender — cool first), blend to a slightly coarse purée. Taste and adjust heat with a reserved chili if desired.
8. Return the purée to the pan. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook 6–7 minutes until it no longer smells raw. Add a splash of hot water if too thick.
9. Add garam masala, fried paneer (save a few cubes for garnish), and cream. Stir well and cook 3–4 more minutes until fragrant.
10. Make the tempering: melt butter in a small pan over low heat. Add sliced garlic and cook 2 minutes — do not brown. Add dried red chili and fry until crisp. Turn off heat. Add hing and chili powder.
11. Pour tempering over the saag paneer. Garnish with reserved paneer. Serve with basmati rice, naan, or roti with a squeeze of lemon.

## Ingredient Substitutions

- **Mustard greens** — baby kale works well; all-spinach also fine (use 8 cups total)
- **Paneer** — at Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, Indian grocery. Do not substitute tofu; texture doesn't work
- **Heavy cream** — cashew cream (blend 12 cashews + ½ cup water) or full-fat coconut cream
- **Hing (asafoetida)** — optional, omit freely; available at Indian grocery or Amazon
- **Kashmiri red chili powder** — regular mild chili flakes work fine
