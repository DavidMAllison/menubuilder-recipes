# Red Boat Pork Belly in Caramel Sauce

## Ingredients

- 1 pound lean pork belly,  with or without skin, or fatty pork shoulder
- 1 1/2 teaspoons Red Boat Salt
- 3/4 teaspoon recently or freshly ground black pepper
- 2 teaspoons neutral oil, such as canola
- 1  small shallot, minced (1 1/2 tablespoons)
- 1  large garlic clove, minced (1 1/2 teaspoons)
- 2 tablespoons white granulated sugar or shaved light palm sugar ((about 1 ounce) )
- 1 tablespoon Red Boat fish sauce
- 2 tablespoons water
- 1  or 2 Thai chilis, split lengthwise
- 1  Persian cucumber, thickly sliced on the diagonal

## Instructions

1. For pork belly, slice perpendicular to the grain into 1 to 1 1/4-inch wide rectangular strips, then cut those strips into 1/4-inch-thick slices. (If using pork shoulder instead, cut it into comparably sized pieces approximately 1/4-inch thick, making sure each piece contains some fat along with the meat.) Sprinkle the pork with salt and pepper, then let it sit.

2. Pour oil into a 3-quart saucepan and heat it to medium until warm. Introduce the shallot and garlic, stirring often for approximately 2 minutes until some bits begin to color. Extinguish the heat.

3. Move the pan away from the heat source and allow the bubbling to subside, then scoop the shallot and garlic into a small bowl or plate. Set this aside where you can reach it easily.

4. Return the pan to medium heat and add the sugar, allowing it to melt while stirring frequently using a silicone spatula. When the sugar becomes melted and develops a light orange caramelized hue, roughly 1 minute has passed—at this point, return the shallot and garlic to the pan. Continue cooking for about 2 minutes more to deepen the browning of the aromatics and intensify the caramelization to a deep dark brown. Reduce heat if the pan seems to be heating too aggressively or if smoke appears.

5. Add all the pork and combine thoroughly. Keep stirring as the meat cooks until it loses its raw appearance, taking around 2 minutes. Note that sugar will adhere to your spatula, so use a spoon or other tool to scrape it off.

6. Pour in the fish sauce, water, and Thai chilis. Mix well and bring to a strong simmer. Put the lid on and reduce heat to maintain a gentle simmer. Allow it to cook for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring from time to time and adjusting heat as necessary. The pork will be ready when it turns tender yet firm, displays a beautiful brown-red tone, and the pan contains a rich, flavorful sauce. Shut off the heat, cover the pan, and allow it to sit undisturbed for 5 minutes so the flavors can blend together.

7. Should excess fat be present (particularly likely with pork belly), use a slotted spoon to move the pork into a separate bowl. Tilt the pan and skim away as much fat as desired using a metal ladle; keep this fat for future cooking needs. Return the pork to the pan and stir to coat it evenly with the sauce.

8. Sample the pork and its sauce. Adjust the seasoning as needed by adding small amounts of Red Boat Salt, fish sauce, pepper, or sugar to achieve a balanced savory, spicy, and sweet profile. Should the dish taste overly salty or if you prefer more sauce, whisk in water one tablespoon at a time, reheating if necessary to blend everything together. Pour into a shallow serving dish and present with the cucumbers offered separately as a side.
