# Tuscan White Bean Soup (Ribollita-style)

**Adapted from**: [Love and Lemons / Feasting at Home](https://www.feastingathome.com/ribollita-recipe/)

## Ingredients

- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for drizzling
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 2 medium carrots, peeled and diced
- 2 celery ribs, diced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 teaspoons chopped fresh rosemary
- 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
- 1/2 cup dry white wine
- 1 (28-ounce) can diced tomatoes
- 4 cups vegetable broth
- 2 (15-ounce) cans cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 Parmesan rind (optional but recommended)
- 1 bunch Tuscan kale (lacinato kale), stems removed and leaves chopped
- 1 1/2 teaspoons kosher salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 4-6 slices crusty bread (ciabatta or sourdough), toasted
- Grated Parmesan cheese for serving

## Before You Begin

- Use stale or day-old crusty bread - fresh bread will get too mushy
- Save Parmesan rinds in the freezer for recipes like this - they add incredible flavor
- This soup tastes even better the next day as flavors develop

## Instructions

1. Heat olive oil in a large Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Add onion, carrots, and celery. Cook, stirring occasionally, until vegetables are softened, about 8-10 minutes.
2. Add garlic, rosemary, and red pepper flakes. Cook, stirring constantly, until fragrant, about 1 minute.
3. Add white wine and cook until mostly reduced, about 2 minutes, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom.
4. Add diced tomatoes with their juices, vegetable broth, cannellini beans, and Parmesan rind (if using). Bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer uncovered for 20 minutes to allow flavors to develop.
5. Remove and discard Parmesan rind. Add chopped kale and cook until wilted and tender, about 5 minutes. Season with salt and pepper to taste.
6. To serve traditionally: Place a piece of toasted bread in the bottom of each bowl, ladle soup over the bread, and drizzle with extra-virgin olive oil. Top with grated Parmesan. Alternatively, serve bread on the side for dipping. The bread will soak up the flavorful broth.
