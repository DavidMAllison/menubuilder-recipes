# Pasta e ceci (Pasta and Chickpeas)

**Time**: 30 minutes  
**Adapted from**: [Memorie di Angelina](https://memoriediangelina.com/2009/09/17/pasta-e-ceci)  

## Notes

- The sauce can run thin on pasta — either reduce pasta slightly or add more broth than the recipe calls for. Adjust to taste next time.

## Ingredients

- 1 clove garlic, slightly crushed
- 1 sprig fresh rosemary
- Olive oil (enough to coat the bottom of the pot)
- 2–3 whole canned tomatoes (e.g., San Marzano), finely chopped
- 1 can (15.5 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 7 oz ditalini or other small soup pasta
- 1 cup water or broth, or enough to cover
- Salt and pepper

**Optional:**
- 2 oz pancetta or bacon, cubed
- 1 peperoncino or chile de árbol
- Small handful fresh parsley, finely chopped
- Grated pecorino or parmesan, for serving

## Instructions

1. If using pancetta or bacon, cook it first in a dry pot over medium heat until the fat renders and it begins to crisp. Then add olive oil, the garlic clove, and rosemary—and if you want, the red pepper and/or parsley. Sauté until fragrant.
2. Before the chickpeas go in, mash or purée about a third of them (a fork, food mill, or small blender all work). When the aroma of the garlic and rosemary begins wafting about, remove and discard them, then add the tomato (add more if you like, depending on how 'red' you want the dish) and simmer for a few minutes.
3. Add all the chickpeas — whole and puréed — then add water or broth to cover and simmer for 5 minutes. The puréed portion will melt into the liquid and thicken the soup.
4. Boil the pasta separately until al dente, then add to the pot. Turn off the heat (add a splash of water if needed), cover, and let rest 3–5 minutes. Serve with a drizzle of olive oil, black pepper, and/or grated cheese.
