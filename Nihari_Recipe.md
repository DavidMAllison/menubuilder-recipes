# Nihari Recipe

## Ingredients

- 2 to 3 (1 gram)   bay leaves
- 1½ tablespoon coriander seed
- 1 teaspoon (3 grams)   black pepper ((more if you want))
- 3 inches (2 grams)  Ceylon cinnamon piece  ((use 1 inch piece if using cassia))
- 6  cloves
- 1 (1 gram)   black cardamom
- 8 (1 gram)   green cardamom
- ¾ teaspoon (2 grams)   shahi jeera ( (Indian caraway))
- ¾ teaspoon (2 grams)   fennel seeds
- 1 (1 gram)  small star anise
- ¼ teaspoon nutmeg
- 2 strands mace  ((use half mace for a stronger flavor))
- ½ teaspoon all-spice ( (kabab chini))
- ¾ inch long pepper ( (pippali))
- ¾ gram (1 portion)   stone flower ( (see photos in the post, omit if you don&#39;t have))
- 1½ tablespoons (1½ grams)   organic dried rose petals ((or use 1 tsp rose water at the garnishing stage))
- ½ inch dried galangal  ( (optional, sub with ground ginger, but different flavor))
- 5 to 6  vetiver roots/khus ((optional))
- ½ kg (1.1 lbs.)  bone-in mutton ((goat or lamb))
- ¼ cup ghee (or mustard oil (read notes))
- 2 cups (250 grams)  onions (sliced thin)
- ¾ tablespoon ginger (grated/paste)
- ¾ tablespoon garlic (pressed/grate/paste)
- 1½ teaspoon Kashmiri red chili powder ((adjust to heat preference, more if you want))
- ½ teaspoon turmeric
- 1½ teaspoon sea salt ((divided, adjust as needed))
- ¼ cup wheat flour (/atta )
- 5 to 6 cups  hot water ((adjust according to your meat, + 1 cup cold water))
- ½ to ¾ cup yogurt (/curd)
- 1 inch ginger julienned
- ¼ cup coriander leaves (/cilantro fine chopped)
- 2  green chilies (sliced)
- 1 large lemon

## Instructions

1. Place all whole spices except rose petals in a pan and toast over low heat for 3 to 5 minutes until fragrant and crispy develop. Move them onto a plate and let them cool fully. Using the same pan, gently heat the rose petals until they're warm to the touch.

2. Use a grinder jar to pulverize the toasted whole spices into a fine powder, reserving 2 tablespoons in a small bowl. Combine the rose petals with the leftover ground spices and grind until you achieve a fine powder. Keep covered until you're ready to use.

3. Warm oil in a deep pot and brown the onions to a light golden shade. Add the mutton and continue to cook on medium heat for 6 to 7 minutes.

4. Stir in the ginger garlic paste and cook for 1 minute until the raw flavor cooks off. Decrease the heat, then mix in the turmeric, red chili powder, salt, and half of the nihari masala powder.

5. Pour in hot (not boiling) water and mix well. Let the mixture bubble gently, place the lid on the pot, and turn the heat down to low. Allow it to simmer until the mutton softens and separates easily from the bone. Timing varies with your heat level: roughly 1 hour 45 minutes on medium heat or 2 hours 45 minutes on low heat.

6. In a small bowl, blend the flour, remaining nihari masala, yogurt, and half of the water together. Keep mixing until the texture is smooth like a slurry, incorporating more water if necessary. Gradually pour this mixture into the nihari while stirring to prevent lumps.

7. Bring back to a boil, cover, and let simmer for 30 minutes until the consistency becomes thick yet pourable with fat visibly pooling on the surface. If the gravy appears too watery, remove the cover and cook on high heat to evaporate excess liquid.

8. Taste the dish and adjust the salt level as desired. Top with sliced green chilies, thin strips of ginger, and fresh coriander leaves chopped finely.

9. Squeeze fresh lemon juice over top and serve alongside Tandoori roti, Naan, or another type of soft or rustic bread.
