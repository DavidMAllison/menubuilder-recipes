# Gua Bao with Braised Pork Belly, Seasoned Daikon, and Hoisin-Soy Sauce

**Time**: 4 hours (mostly hands-off; bao rise adds ~1 hour)
**Servings**: 4–6

## Ingredients

**Braised Pork Belly:**
- 1 (2-lb) skin-on center-cut pork belly, about 1½ inches thick
- 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
- 3 cups water
- ¼ cup Shaoxing wine or dry sherry
- 3 tablespoons each: hoisin, soy sauce, oyster sauce, packed brown sugar
- 3 whole dried arbol chiles
- 3 garlic cloves, smashed
- 2 star anise pods
- 1 (1-inch) piece ginger, peeled and sliced

**Seasoned Daikon:**
- 8 oz daikon, peeled, halved, and sliced ⅛ inch thick
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- ½ teaspoon salt

**Hoisin-Soy Sauce:**
- ¼ cup hoisin sauce
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce

**Bao Dough:**
- 2½ cups (12½ oz) all-purpose flour
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 1¾ teaspoons instant yeast
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- ½ teaspoon salt
- 1 cup warm whole milk (100°F)
- 2 tablespoons vegetable shortening
- ¼ teaspoon plus 2 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided

**Garnishes:**
- 1¼ cups fresh cilantro leaves and stems
- 3 scallions, halved and cut into 2-inch pieces
- ⅓ cup dry-roasted peanuts, chopped
- 2 Thai chiles, minced (optional)

## Instructions

**Pork Belly:**
1. Slice pork into ¾-inch-wide strips, then cut crosswise into 16 pieces about 2–2½ inches long. Heat oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high. Brown the pork in a single layer, turning halfway, until golden, 15–20 minutes.
2. Combine water, Shaoxing wine, hoisin, soy sauce, oyster sauce, and sugar in a bowl; add to the pot along with the chiles, garlic, star anise, and ginger. Bring to a boil over high, then cover and simmer vigorously until the pork is tender and a fork slides in easily, about 1 hour.
3. Transfer pork to a bowl with a slotted spoon. Pour the liquid into a fat separator and let sit 5 minutes; discard solids. Return the defatted liquid to the pot and simmer over medium until reduced to about 1 cup, about 15 minutes. Return pork to the pot, cover, and keep warm. (Can be refrigerated up to 2 days; reheat gently.)

**Daikon:**
4. Toss daikon with sugar and salt in a bowl. Cover and refrigerate until needed (up to 24 hours).

**Hoisin-Soy:**
5. Stir hoisin and soy sauce together in a small bowl.

**Bao:**
6. In a stand mixer with a dough hook, combine flour, sugar, yeast, baking powder, and salt on low until mixed. Add milk and shortening; mix until no dry flour remains, about 2 minutes. Increase to medium-low and knead until smooth and pulling away from the sides, 5 minutes. Form into a ball, coat with ¼ teaspoon oil in a bowl, cover, and let rise until doubled, about 1 hour.
7. Cut sixteen 4-inch parchment squares and divide between 2 baking sheets. Pour remaining 2 tablespoons oil into a small bowl. Press dough to deflate, knead briefly, then divide in half. Roll one half into an 8-inch log and cut into 8 equal pieces. For each piece: pinch edges to the center and shape into a smooth ball. Roll into a 6-inch oval, brush lightly with oil, fold in half, and transfer to a parchment square. Repeat with remaining dough. Cover and let rise until slightly puffy, about 30 minutes.
8. Bring 4 cups water to a boil in a wide wok or skillet. Arrange buns in two 10-inch bamboo steamer baskets (4 per basket), on their parchment squares. Stack baskets, cover, and set over the simmering water. Steam 10 minutes without lifting the lid. Off heat, rest covered for 5 minutes. Transfer to a rack. Steam remaining buns.
9. To serve, tuck a slice of pork into each bun and garnish with daikon, hoisin-soy sauce, cilantro, scallions, peanuts, and chiles if using.

## Notes

- Ask the butcher for a flat, rectangular section of skin-on belly with roughly equal meat and fat.
- Bao keep at room temperature for 3 hours or refrigerated for 3 days; re-steam 2–3 minutes to serve. Freeze for up to 1 month; steam from frozen 7–8 minutes.
- Make pork and garnishes first, then tackle the bao dough.
