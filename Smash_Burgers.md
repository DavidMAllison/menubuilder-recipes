# Smash Burgers

**Time**: 20 minutes  
**Yield**: Makes 4 burgers

## Notes

- Smashing is a one-time move — do it the moment the beef hits the surface, before it starts to cook.
- The double-patty stack with one slice of cheese is the classic form. For single patties, use 3–4 oz per patty.
- Same technique works for lamb burgers — lamb renders more fat so watch for flare-ups. Use the cast iron sear tray on the Weber rather than the grill grates directly.

## Ingredients

- 1 lb (450g) freshly ground beef chuck (80/20), divided into 8 (2 oz) balls
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 4 slices American cheese (or sharp cheddar)
- 4 soft hamburger buns, buttered and toasted

**Toppings (as desired)**
- Mayonnaise, yellow mustard, shredded lettuce, sliced tomato, onion, pickles

## Equipment Note

You need a flat surface to smash against. On the grill, place a cast iron griddle or flat-top grill insert directly on the grates and preheat thoroughly — a screaming hot surface is essential for the crust.

## Before You Begin

- Assemble all toppings on buns before you start cooking — these burgers go from grill to bun in under 2 minutes.
- Use a stiff metal spatula for smashing and scraping. A second spatula or metal bench scraper adds leverage for the smash.
- Cook one burger at a time; tent finished burgers with foil while you work through the rest.
- Use loose balls of meat, not pre-formed patties — loose balls smash and sear better.
- 80/20 ground chuck gives the best crust and flavor. Leaner beef won't develop the same sear.

## Instructions

1. Preheat grill to high. Place cast iron griddle or flat-top insert on grates and heat for 5 minutes until very hot.
2. Prepare buns: add desired condiments and toppings to the bottom half of each bun. Set aside.
3. Place 2 balls of beef on the hot griddle. Immediately smash each ball firmly into a thin patty slightly wider than the bun, using a stiff metal spatula and a second spatula or bench scraper for extra pressure.
4. Season generously with salt and pepper. Cook until well browned on the bottom and edges are beginning to look cooked, about 45 seconds to 1 minute.
5. Scrape patties up with the spatula, getting all the browned bits. Flip both patties. Immediately place a slice of cheese on one patty and stack the second patty directly on top.
6. Transfer the double-patty stack to a waiting bun immediately. Serve.
7. Repeat for remaining burgers, tenting finished ones with foil.
